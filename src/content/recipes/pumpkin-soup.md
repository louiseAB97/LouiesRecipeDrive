First cut the pumpkin with a large knife so that you create flat surfaces for a firm grip while cutting. Scoop out the seeds and stringy flesh with a spoon and throw them away. Then, if the pumpkin has bumps or scar-like patches on the skin, cut those bits of skin away. The rest of the skin can stay on. Put a full kettle of water on. Cut the pumpkin into chunks of 2–4 cm; this doesn't need to be precise. Chop the red onion and fry it in two tablespoons of good olive oil for 2 minutes. Then add the pumpkin pieces and fry over medium heat for a few more minutes, until at least a few pieces are slightly browned. When it starts to risk burning, add the hot water until the pumpkin is covered and crumble a stock cube into it. Now let it boil for about 20 min with the lid on. Then check whether the pumpkin falls apart when you prick it with a fork. Yes? > grab the blender, turn off the heat and purée the soup (keep blending for a good two minutes; the soup should be really smooth).

Serve with toasted slices of ciabatta and a peeled garlic clove. Rub the garlic clove over the bread and drizzle good olive oil over it above your soup. And a little extra salt.
