For the dashi stock:  
Make a few small snips in the kombu. Put a small pan on with the water and the kombu and let it barely simmer for 10 min (really don't let it boil, or it turns bitter and slimy). Then remove the kombu, add the bonito flakes and now let it actually boil for a minute or so. Turn off the heat and let it settle.

For the roux (you can also buy ready-made curry roux blocks):  
Cook the flour and butter for a few minutes over low heat, stirring regularly with a wooden spatula, until it turns a light caramel colour. Then add salt, 3 g (Japanese) curry powder and optionally a sprinkle of garam masala. Cook for a few more seconds and the roux is ready to use.

For the breaded tofu:  
Carefully cut the tofu into cubes and coat them in a batter of flour, milk and salt with the consistency of pancake batter, then roll them in panko. It's a bit of a jiggly job. Deep-fry in a wok in sunflower oil.

For the soup, cut the white onion into fairly thick vertical half-rings and stir-fry in a little sunflower oil. Add the carrot and the mushrooms. Keep it all short so it still has some bite. Turn off the heat. Meanwhile, cook the noodles in another pan exactly according to the packet instructions, then drain immediately and rinse for 2 seconds under cold water so they don't overcook. Divide the noodles over the plates. Then add the dashi to the vegetables and stir in the roux well. Ladle the broth over the noodles and garnish with the fried tofu, chilli and coriander. Not really authentic but tasty: a wedge of lime on the side.
